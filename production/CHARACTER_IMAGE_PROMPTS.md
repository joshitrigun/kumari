## King Jayaprakash Malla - Penance (Prayer to Taleju's Temple)

**The beat (for the storyteller):** He does not fall into rags. He falls into *longing*. The game is over, the goddess has withdrawn, and the man who once sat across from her as an equal now cannot reach her at all — so he does the only thing a devotee can do: he prays *toward* her. Every dawn the tall king climbs to the carved window of his own palace and looks out across the brick square to the tiered Taleju temple, the sanctum where her presence still lives and where he is no longer welcome as he once was. He keeps his royal dignity — this is a king humbled, not a king destroyed — but strips away the pride: no towering headpiece, no gold, just plain cotton and folded hands. The whole distance between palace and temple is the distance between them now. His prayer is the covenant continued in the only form left to him: reverence across an unbridgeable space. This is penance as devotion, not degradation.

`Traditional Newar paubha-painting-style illustration, 18th-century Kathmandu Valley, Nepal. A tall, dignified 50-year-old Nepali Newar Malla king stands at the carved wooden window (ankhijhyal) of his palace terrace at dawn, seen in three-quarter view from slightly behind, gazing out across the red-brick Durbar square toward the towering multi-tiered pagoda Taleju temple in the distance. He is humbled but not broken: no royal regalia, no headpiece, no gold — only simple undyed cream cotton robes and a plain sash, dark hair loosely tied, a single restrained mark of station. Still strong and handsome, his face carries quiet grief and unbroken longing, eyes fixed on the temple. Both hands are raised and pressed together at his chest in devotional prayer (namaskar mudra). Soft misty dawn light; the temple's golden roof-finials catch the first light; thin threads of incense rise between the palace and the distant shrine; a few pigeons on the brick. Flat mineral pigment, gold-leaf accents on the temple, bold ink outlines, ornate painted architecture — reverent, sorrowful, mythic, never photoreal.`

## Approval Rule

Reject any result that looks like:

- Malaysian songket or tengkolok/songkok.
- Thai or Balinese ceremonial silhouette.
- Mughal/Rajput fantasy palace character.
- Generic anime/manga, Disney/princess design, or 3D Pixar look.
- Flat corporate vector/SVG logo or cartoon mascot (paubha is *painted*, not vector-flat).
- Photoreal photograph or live-action still (we now want paubha painting, not realism).
- Glamour goddess or romantic Taleju.
- Cute or spooky Kumari.

Approve only if it reads as a genuine Newar **paubha / thangka painting** — hand-painted mineral pigment, ink outlines, sacred iconography — *and* a Nepali/Newar viewer can immediately feel Kathmandu Valley / Newar / Malla influence.
